## What I found 

Since my first blog entry, I have started researching how technology is used in soccer. I looked into the **Video Assistant Referee (VAR)** system and **goal-line technology**. According to the [IFAB’s VAR Protocol](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/video-assistant-referee-var-protocol/), VAR reviews certain important match decisions, but the referee on the field makes the final decision. I also learned from [IFAB’s information about goal-line technology](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-referee/) that the system helps officials determine whether the whole ball has crossed the goal line. It must send its confirmation quickly and automatically.

I searched for information about VAR and goal-line technology, then focused on IFAB because it is responsible for the Laws of the Game. Using an official source helped me find reliable explanations of how these technologies work. I still want to research player-tracking and performance-analysis tools, because they could show how technology helps players and coaches.

## How I organized 

So far, I have grouped the technologies by their purpose: **helping referees make decisions** and **helping players or coaches evaluate performance**. This organization makes sense for a website aimed at high school students because it shows that soccer technology can affect different people in the sport. As I find more examples, I may adjust these categories.

## Engineering Design Process

I am currently in the research stage of the **engineering design process**. I have begun identifying the problem my website will address: high school students may not know how technology is used in soccer or how it could affect the sport in the future. My next step is to research more examples, especially player-performance tools, and decide which information will be most helpful to my audience.
